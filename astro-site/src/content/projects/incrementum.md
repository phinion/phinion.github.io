## Overview

Incrementum was a 3D psychological thriller created during my second-year **Group Project** module at Bournemouth University.

This was my first experience developing a game as part of a dedicated multidisciplinary team. Our group consisted of six students: three software engineers and three designers.

We were given the freedom to create a game of our choosing, and together we decided to explore the psychological thriller genre, building an experience centred around mystery, exploration and environmental puzzles.

## The Game

Incrementum follows a character investigating the mysterious mansion of **Dr Nikolov Zemia**, searching for clues about what happened there.

Starting on the first floor, players gradually make their way deeper into the mansion, exploring rooms and solving puzzles to progress.

The experience culminates in the basement, where the player must find a way to escape through a hatch.

We wanted to create a sense of mystery and tension as players uncovered more of the mansion, using environmental design, sound and interactive puzzles to drive the experience forward.

## My Contributions

My primary responsibility was **gameplay programming**, although I also contributed to group discussions around the game's overall design and direction.

Working in Unity and C#, I implemented several core gameplay features:

- **First-Person Controller:** Developed the player's movement and navigation mechanics for exploring the mansion.
- **Inventory System:** Implemented a system for managing items collected during exploration.
- **Flashlight Mechanic:** Added a usable flashlight to support exploration through the mansion.
- **Puzzle Development:** Created interactive puzzles that players needed to solve to progress through the game.
- **Asset Integration:** Worked with our designers to bring their artwork and game assets into Unity.

One of my favourite features was an electrical circuit puzzle inspired by the puzzle mechanics in *Marvel's Spider-Man* on PlayStation 4.

Players had to rotate different connection points to create a continuous path for an electrical current to travel from one side of the puzzle to the other.

This was a particularly enjoyable challenge, as it involved translating a puzzle concept into an interactive gameplay system.

## Working as a Team

Incrementum taught me a lot about collaborative game development.

Our three designers were responsible for creating the game's art assets, while the engineering team worked on implementing the gameplay systems and integrating those assets into Unity.

We also recorded our own voice lines and used freely available music assets to support the atmosphere of the game. The artwork and programming were created by our team.

I was involved in discussions about the game's direction and worked closely with my teammates as we developed the experience.

It was also my first real experience navigating disagreements during a game project.

I learned the importance of listening to different perspectives, resolving creative differences and making decisions that helped the team move forward.

We also discovered how quickly development schedules could become difficult to manage, particularly when multiple people were working towards the same deadline.

Despite those challenges, we pulled together and delivered a playable experience with a beginning, progression through the mansion and a conclusion.

## Reflections

Incrementum was one of the most valuable projects from my early university experience.

While I'd already built games independently, this was the first time I had to consider how my programming work fit into a larger team effort.

It helped me develop my communication skills, understand the importance of realistic deadlines and learn how to collaborate with designers to bring a shared vision to life.

Although there are plenty of things I would improve with the experience I have today, I'm proud of what our team managed to create.
