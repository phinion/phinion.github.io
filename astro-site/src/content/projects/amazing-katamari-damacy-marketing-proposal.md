## Overview

As part of my second-year Commercial and Business Environment module at Bournemouth University, our team was tasked with developing a simulated marketing proposal for a mobile game of our choosing.

We chose Bandai Namco's *Amazing Katamari Damacy*, a game whose quirky personality gave us plenty of opportunities to explore unconventional marketing ideas.

The project involved researching the commercial side of the games industry, developing a marketing strategy and presenting our proposal through a seven-minute video pitch.

## My Role — Project Manager

I took on the role of project manager, leading a team of six games software engineering and design students, including myself.

My responsibilities included:

- Planning and organising the project's development using an Agile approach.
- Delegating tasks based on individual strengths and abilities.
- Coordinating the team and keeping our work on schedule.
- Supporting the creation of promotional materials and marketing concepts.
- Contributing to the script and creative direction of our final presentation.

We worked together to develop promotional concepts including a desktop website, mobile website and poster artwork.

We also explored real-world promotional events, such as pop-up stalls featuring zorb balls, allowing visitors to experience the feeling of rolling around and collecting objects like a Katamari.

## Video Production & Creative Direction

Rather than delivering a conventional business presentation, we wanted our submission to capture the humour and personality of the game.

We created a seven-minute video pitching our marketing proposal directly to the King of All Cosmos, a character from the Katamari series.

I edited the final presentation using Adobe Premiere Pro, incorporating music from the game and animating my teammates' faces onto Katamari characters, synchronising their movements with the recorded dialogue.

We also had the opportunity to produce a separate two-minute promotional trailer.

For this, we created a deliberately ridiculous live-action Katamari skit, which involved me rolling around the university computer labs and collecting objects along the way.

It was a great opportunity to combine creative storytelling, humour and video editing with the more formal business requirements of the assignment.

## Outcome & What I Learned

Our project was awarded a **First**, something I'm particularly proud of given the amount of collaboration and creativity involved.

More importantly, the experience helped me discover how much I enjoyed leading a team.

I learned how to recognise people's strengths, distribute responsibilities effectively and keep a group working towards a shared goal.

This remains one of my favourite university projects, not just because of the final result, but because of how much fun we had creating it together.
