## Overview

The Tomb of Anubis was a first-year university project developed for my **Games Design Principles** module at Bournemouth University.

The assignment involved researching game design frameworks, analysing existing tabletop games and applying what we had learned to create an original board or card game.

This was my first collaborative project at university, working alongside a fellow student who would eventually become one of my closest friends.

Together, we designed and created a physical tabletop game inspired by Egyptian mythology, combining elements of classic board games with competitive card-based mechanics.

## Game Design

We wanted to create something that was easy to understand but still offered opportunities for competition and strategy.

The foundation was inspired by *Snakes and Ladders*, with players progressing across a board designed to resemble an Egyptian pyramid.

However, we introduced an additional mechanic inspired by games we both enjoyed, particularly *Yu-Gi-Oh!*, *UNO* and *Top Trumps*.

Landing on certain tiles would trigger a card battle between players.

Each card featured different attributes, including:

- **Attack**
- **Defence**
- **Speed**

Players would compare these values during encounters, adding a competitive element to what would otherwise be a straightforward board game.

The cards were themed around Egyptian mythology, with names and artwork designed to complement the pyramid setting.

Our goal was to combine familiar mechanics into something with its own identity.

## Designing & Building the Game

Unlike my programming projects, this assignment was entirely focused on game design and physical prototyping.

We developed the game's rules, designed the board and cards, and created a design document explaining our decisions using the principles covered in lectures.

We also produced a physical version of the game, printing the board and individual cards ourselves.

This turned out to be considerably more expensive than expected, costing us over £60. In hindsight, we probably should have used the university's printing facilities!

We even improvised our player counters using pieces of cut-up crayons.

Although the production process was a little chaotic, it was a fun, hands-on experience that allowed us to see our ideas become something people could actually play.

## Reflections

The Tomb of Anubis was one of my earliest experiences with collaborative game design.

It helped me appreciate the importance of establishing clear rules, thinking about player interactions and creating engaging mechanics without relying on technology.

It was also a reminder that game development doesn't always have to involve writing code. Sometimes all you need is an idea, some cards, a board and a willingness to experiment.

The finished game is probably still sitting somewhere in my parents' garage, and I'd love to revisit it one day.
