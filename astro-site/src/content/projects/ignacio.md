## Overview

Ignacio was a first-year university project developed for my **Games Development Pipeline** module at Bournemouth University.

The assignment was to create a simple 2D platformer using Construct, a game development tool that made it easy to experiment with gameplay mechanics without needing to build everything from scratch.

It reminded me of the projects I used to create in Scratch when I was younger, and it was a fun opportunity to focus on the creative side of game development.

While the assignment only required a platformer with a few levels, I wanted to take it further by introducing additional mechanics, challenging level design and a proper boss encounter.

## Development

I designed and developed three challenging levels, focusing on movement, timing and avoiding enemy projectiles.

One of my favourite additions was the **Metroidvania-inspired camera system**. Rather than switching instantly between rooms, the screen would smoothly slide into the next area when the player reached the edge, making exploration feel more connected.

Some of the key features I implemented included:

- **Platforming Mechanics:** Player movement, jumping and double jumping to navigate the levels.
- **Level Design:** Three challenging levels featuring environmental obstacles and enemy turrets that fired projectiles at the player.
- **Screen Transitions:** Smooth, Metroidvania-style transitions between connected areas.
- **Combat System:** A simple punching mechanic used during the final boss encounter.
- **Boss Fight:** A boss with basic AI that attacked the player using different projectiles.
- **Projectile Parrying:** Players could reflect the boss's green projectiles back towards it, turning its own attacks into a way to deal damage.
- **Presentation:** A title screen and cutscene to give the game a more complete experience.

These additions gave me an opportunity to experiment with gameplay mechanics beyond the original assignment requirements.

## Reflections

Ignacio was one of my favourite early university projects because it allowed me to focus on **game design and creating a fun player experience**, rather than purely on programming.

Although the game wasn't particularly polished, I'm proud of how much I managed to include and how I experimented with different mechanics to create something more interesting than a basic platformer.

Looking back, it's also a reminder of how much I enjoyed making games when I was younger, experimenting with ideas in Scratch and seeing what I could create.

Who knows? Maybe I'll revisit Ignacio one day and turn it into a more complete game.
