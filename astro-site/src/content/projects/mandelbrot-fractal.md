# Mandelbrot Fractal

This project was developed during my first year at university as part of the **Maths for Computer Graphics** module. It was one of my first experiences with graphical programming, using **C++ and SDL** to render 2D shapes and explore mathematical concepts visually.

At this point in the course, we'd only recently started learning C++ through console applications, including a Blackjack game, while also working on a simple SDL game. This assignment introduced us to rendering shapes using matrices and gave me my first real understanding of how computer graphics worked.

## Going Beyond the Assignment

The original goal was fairly straightforward: render a triangle and then a square using C++ and SDL.

I've always enjoyed maths, so I found myself having a lot of fun experimenting with what else I could create. Rather than stopping at the required shapes, I expanded the application into an interactive program where users could choose what to render from a numbered menu.

The application eventually supported:

- Lines, triangles, squares and rectangles
- Sine, cosine and tangent waves
- Circles and animated circles
- Mandelbrot fractal generation

I also added **input validation** to handle user selections and experimented with filling shapes as I learnt more about rendering.

## Discovering the Mandelbrot Set

One of the most exciting parts of the project was learning about the **Mandelbrot set** and how mathematical equations could produce such intricate patterns.

I decided to take the project further by implementing my own Mandelbrot fractal renderer, generating colourful patterns through iterative calculations.

I also introduced basic magnification controls, allowing users to specify a zoom level alongside X and Y offsets to explore different parts of the fractal.

Rendering the fractal could take a considerable amount of time, especially at higher magnifications, so these controls allowed users to choose which region they wanted to explore before generating the image.

## What I Took Away

This project was a particularly memorable introduction to computer graphics. It helped me understand how mathematics, programming and rendering work together to create visual results.

It was also one of my first experiences taking a relatively simple assignment and expanding it into something much more ambitious purely out of curiosity.

I received a **First** for the project, which was incredibly rewarding, but what I remember most is how much fun I had experimenting and discovering what I could build.

Looking back, it was an early example of something I still enjoy today: taking an idea, experimenting with it, and pushing it further than originally planned.
