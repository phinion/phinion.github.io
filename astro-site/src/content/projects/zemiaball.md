## Overview

ZemiaBall was a second-year university project developed for my **Programming for Interaction** module at Bournemouth University.

The assignment was to create a simple game in Unity that explored player interaction. I decided to build a 3D platformer inspired by games such as *Super Monkey Ball* and *Super Mario 64*.

The game was designed to be played on PC using a controller, with an emphasis on movement, jumping and collecting coins within a time limit.

## Gameplay & Development

I started by creating a series of tutorial levels to introduce the player to movement and jumping before progressing to the main level.

Some of the key mechanics included:

- **Platforming:** Player movement, jumping and double jumping to navigate the environment.
- **Camera Controls:** Rather than giving the player full camera control, the left and right bumper buttons rotated the camera in 90-degree increments.
- **Collectibles:** Coins scattered throughout the level for players to collect.
- **Timed Challenges:** A time limit that encouraged players to complete the level efficiently.
- **Controller Support:** Gameplay designed around using a controller on PC.

The main level took inspiration from the opening level of *Super Mario 64*, with an island-like environment designed around exploration and platforming.

The camera system was particularly unconventional. Looking back, I think it was partly an attempt to make the game feel different and partly because I was still learning how to implement 3D camera controls!

Although the mechanics were relatively straightforward, it was a valuable opportunity to experiment with player interaction and learn more about building 3D games in Unity.

## Reflections

Looking back at ZemiaBall with the experience I have today, there are plenty of things I could improve or expand upon.

But at the time, I was still discovering so much about Unity and game development. Every new mechanic was another opportunity to learn something.

I also included music from the *Pokémon Diamond and Pearl: Battle Dimension* anime series as the title theme.

Amusingly, I'd used the same music in a Scratch project I made 15 years ago!

I think that's one of the things I love most about making games. Even as I've grown as a developer, I still find inspiration in the same games, music and experiences that excited me as a child.

That sense of curiosity and wonder has never really gone away, and I hope it continues to influence the things I create.

ZemiaBall might be a simple university project, but it's one I'm still fond of.
